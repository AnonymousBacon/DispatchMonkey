C1 · Problem, user, decision — 20 pts

A truck can be on time and still lose the morning

Four things must line up for one container: cargo ready, truck free, travel conditions, warehouse receiving window. When one changes, the dispatcher loses track of which deliveries are at risk.

Trucks cannot see each other's plans, so they arrive in the same 2–3 hours and idle in line at the gate.

Intended user

The trucking dispatcher — one fleet, 12 trucks in the demo.

The decision we support

When each truck departs, and which route it takes — scored as one pair, not two separate choices.

Who pays for waiting

Drivers (paid per trip, so queue time is unpaid) · the fleet (diesel, missed appointments) · the neighbourhood and the school on Nguyễn Thị Định (exhaust at the kerb).

C1 · Why this is the right lever

The bottleneck is the gate, not the road

A faster route gets you to the same line sooner. So we schedule arrival, the way a warehouse schedules a dock appointment — and when a slot fills, the next truck is told a different time.

Routing app

One vehicle. Fastest wins. No cargo, no appointment, no queue.

Port booking

Gives you a window. Does not tell you when to leave the depot to reach it without waiting.

DispatchMonkey

Scores 107 departure × route pairs for a 12-truck morning against the queue it is itself creating.

C2 · Response to change — 20 pts

What you will watch change

  • Normal morning. 12 trips. Each row shows when the truck reaches the gate and when the warehouse expects it.
  • A driver reports a crash on cầu Phú Mỹ → the map marks it UNVERIFIED. The plan does not move on one report.
  • A second driver confirms → CORROBORATED (2/3) → a third → CONFIRMED. A driver reporting it clear sends it to EXPIRED.
  • The disruption lands: simulated bridge speed drops, 2 trucks are projected to miss their warehouse windows, fleet idling 71 min.
  • Re-plan. Both windows met, idling 24 min. Trucks already on the road are not moved. Latency 2.7 ms.
  • A confirmed full closure removes that route from the choice set — the card goes dashed and names the rule that banned it.
  • Approve → the state revision increments → the driver's phone redraws in ~1.5 s with the new departure time.

C5 · How each indicator was calculated

No indicator without its formula

  • K1 VND = Σ idle fuel (wait h × 3 L/h × 26,820 ₫/L, MoIT retail 12 Nov 2026) + Σ paid wait for hourly drivers (60,000 ₫/h) + 500,000 ₫ per missed appointment SIM
  • K2 idle truck-min = Σ gate-queue wait, from the queue recurrence Q[t+1] = max(0, Q[t] + A_bg[t]·surge + A_fleet[t] − C[t]), wait = Q / C × 15 min, C = 210 trucks / 15 min. A_fleet includes our own trucks — that is the anti-herding term.
  • K3 unpaid min = for per-trip drivers: queue wait + minutes pushed later than planned + minutes stuck in incident traffic. Max over drivers.
  • K6 window hit rate = % trucks at the dock before the receiving window closes (gate + wait + 30 min pick-up + drive back), 10-min dock buffer.
  • K8 school exposure = Σ wait × 24 % (share of Nguyễn Thị Định within 150 m of a school or clinic, measured from OSM) × 3 during school-bell hours.
  • K10 idle CO₂ = idle h × 3 L/h × 2.68 kg/L.

C3 · Ethical AI & responsible design — 15 pts

Five considerations; two were required

Fairness & stakeholder impact

Worst-off unpaid driver guard (a re-plan may never leave them worse off) · 45-min shift-creep cap, 0 violations · school-exposure minutes are an allocator input, not a footnote · one phone is one source: the reporter cannot confirm their own report, enforced on the server.

Environmental responsibility

Idle CO₂ 9.5 → 3.3 kg and idling next to a school 19 → 7 min are reported for every plan, so a cheaper plan that pushes exhaust onto a residential street is visible before it is approved.

Human oversight

Nothing reaches a driver without the dispatcher pressing Approve. Every plan can be overridden with a written reason, and every driver report carries I see it too / It is clear now / Reply.

Transparency

Every figure shows its formula and its tier. The route line is badged PREVIEW where 4.5 km of 16.8 km is a straight-line stand-in, because the OSM extract has no Thủ Thiêm tunnel way.

Responsible data use

A driver is a random per-phone id, never a name or a login. Posts carry a road name and a direction, never coordinates. Photos are redrawn through a canvas, dropping EXIF GPS. All asserted in tests.

What we refuse to claim

Radar sees ships, not trucks. A photo is badged captured in app, never verified. One driver report is never a road closure. No clip was ever transcribed, so we make no Vietnamese speech-accuracy claim. The model never produces a number — the deterministic engine computes every figure.

Fairness + environment (both required) + transparency + oversight + data use5:50–7:00 — Speaker 4
C6 · Limitations & next steps — 15 pts

What this is not, yet

Limits, stated plainly

  • The fleet, jobs, gate capacity and warehouse windows are simulated. No public Cát Lái gate wait-time data exists.
  • Four live providers are built and fixture-tested but keyless here; each returns a labelled 503.
  • Lane guidance is one segment, not a network. Six further segments sit in lane_rules.json under needs_evidence and produce no prompt at all; the decisive decree is paywalled.
  • 23 of 33 vessels are circled — touching detections merge into one box.
  • 4.5 km of the drawn route is a straight-line stand-in (missing tunnel way).
  • One untuned constant, PAIR_SCALE_MIN = 6, prices route change against fleet queueing.
  • Mobile was verified in emulated Chrome at 390×844 — no physical handset, no real GPS, no recorded speech.

Next steps

  • Pilot with one trucking company — real gate and trip logs are the only way to test whether these simulated indicators match Cát Lái.
  • Train the queue model on real gate throughput instead of the published ≈19k trucks/day estimate.
  • VIETMAP + TomTom Orbis keys → provider geometry and real incident payloads replace the preview stitch.
  • The text of Quyết định 23/2018/QĐ-UBND, to settle the six unsourced lane segments.
  • Design the driver page with actual drivers, on real handsets.
  • Port-level slot booking, so the gate operator and the fleets share one schedule.

Appendix · Q&A, ~20 s per answer

Quick answers

Is any of this real? — S3

Roads, schools and clinics come from OpenStreetMap; ship counts come from real Sentinel-1 images. The trucks and gate operations are simulated.

Doesn't the port already have booking? — S1

A booking gives you a window. We help the dispatcher decide when each truck should leave so it can reach that window with less waiting.

Can 12 trucks fix traffic at a whole port? — S3

With 12 we show how one fleet could wait less. The adoption slider simulates what might happen if more fleets use it.

How accurate is it? — S4

These are simulation results. A pilot with real gate and trip logs would let us test how well they match Cát Lái.

Where's the AI? — S2

Image processing counts ships in radar scenes, and a saved driver-report example reads a Vietnamese message. The scheduling engine calculates every number you see — the model never does.

How do you protect drivers? — S4

Random per-phone ids, unpaid waiting tracked, a cap on how far a departure can be pushed back, and the dispatcher reviews the plan before it goes out.
