01 Intro and hook / Speaker 1

“Imagine getting to Cát Lái port on time, then spending part of your morning waiting in a truck line. For a driver paid by the trip, that can mean unpaid time. For the fleet, it can mean wasted fuel and a missed warehouse appointment. We built DispatchMonkey as a decision-support tool for the dispatcher — the user who has to decide when each truck should leave, based on what’s happening at the gate. Here’s what that looks like from the driver’s seat.”

“What happens if an app tells every driver to leave at the same quiet time? They all show up together, and the jam moves to a new time.”

“In our simulation, the longest line was 239 trucks with no app. When 30 percent of drivers used an app that gave everyone the same time, it grew to 1,218 trucks.”

“DispatchMonkey schedules trucks one at a time. It counts each truck it has already scheduled before choosing a time for the next one. With the same 30 percent using DispatchMonkey, the longest line was 34 trucks. This is one of the indicators we use to test the scenario, and it’s calculated the same way for every bar on this chart: trucks queued at the gate at adoption’s peak minute.”

“We also used radar from 115 satellite passes to count ships through clouds — remote sensing feeding a logistics model. Those counts give us a clue about when more trucks might arrive. That’s two fields working together, not just sitting next to each other: the ship count changes which hours the scheduler treats as high-arrival. Now I’ll hand it over to our next speaker.”

04B Fleet and community benefits / Speaker 4 / 5:50–7:00

“In our simulated crash, idling near schools and clinics fell from 19 minutes to 7 — that’s environmental and community impact, one of our two required responsible-design considerations, and it’s an input to the schedule, not a slide we added afterward. Less engine idling also means less fuel burned.”

“The second is fairness and stakeholder impact: we track the driver with the most unpaid time, not the average, and we limit how far back any one driver’s departure can be moved. We’re also transparent about what we don’t know — every number on screen is tagged real, simulated, or estimated, so no one mistakes a simulated gate for a measured one.”

“The dispatcher can approve the plan or change it and record why — that’s human oversight again, at the last step this time. The demo combines real map and radar data with simulated fleet and gate operations. Our next step is to test this with a real trucking company. DispatchMonkey helps dispatchers choose when each truck should leave. Thank you.”

DISPATCHMONKEYQUESTION AND ANSWER

Q&A quick answers

7:00–9:00  /  Aim for about 20 seconds per answer
Speaker 3
Is any of this real?
“Yes. The roads, schools, and clinics come from OpenStreetMap, and the ship counts come from real satellite images — that’s our real data tier. The trucks and gate operations in the demo are simulated, and we say so on screen rather than letting a judge assume otherwise.”
Speaker 1
Doesn’t the port already have booking?
“A booking gives you a window. We help the dispatcher make the decision of when each truck should leave so it can reach that window with less waiting — booking and DispatchMonkey answer different questions.”
Speaker 3
Can 12 trucks fix traffic at the whole port?
“With 12 trucks, we can show how one fleet could spend less time waiting. The slider simulates what might happen if more fleets use the app — we don’t claim the 12-truck result scales, we show the assumption and let you move it.”
Speaker 4
How accurate is it?
“These are simulation results — an explicit limitation, not a hidden one. A pilot with real gate and trip logs would let us test how well they match what happens at Cát Lái.”
Speaker 2
Where’s the AI?
“We use a saved driver-report example in the demo and image processing to count ships in radar images. The scheduling engine — not the model — calculates the numbers you see. That’s a deliberate responsible-design choice: the AI never gets to invent a figure.”
Speaker 4
How do you protect drivers?
“We use driver IDs, never names or locations; we track unpaid waiting; and we cap how far a driver’s departure can be pushed back. The dispatcher reviews the plan before it goes out — fairness and human oversight together.”
